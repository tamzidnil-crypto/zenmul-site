Employer claims / Article 2

Quit, fired or laid off.

How the reason for a separation shapes an unemployment claim.

Unemployment benefits are meant for people who are out of work through no fault of their own. Each state writes its own rules, but the reason the job ended is almost always the center of the decision. Knowing which category a separation falls into tells you whether a claim is worth contesting at all.

Laid off

A layoff, a downsizing or a closed location usually qualifies for benefits. The person did not choose to leave and did nothing wrong. Contesting a genuine layoff rarely makes sense. An accurate response is enough.

Quit

Every state disqualifies people who quit, unless they had good cause. Each state defines good cause in its own way, and some require the reason to be connected to the employer. When someone quits, the burden is generally on that person to show good cause.

Two things to watch. First, if the employee was told to resign or be fired, many states will not treat that as a voluntary quit. Second, a big change in pay, hours or duties can sometimes count as good cause. Describe what actually happened, not just the label.

Fired

Being fired does not by itself stop someone from collecting benefits. A person fired for misconduct, as the state defines it, may be disqualified. Examples states commonly treat as misconduct include insubordination, repeated unexcused absences after warnings, or coming to work under the influence. In discharge cases, many states put the burden on the employer to prove the misconduct.

What decides the outcome

  • The real reason for the separation, described with dates
  • Written warnings and the policy that was broken, if there was one
  • A resignation message, if the person quit
  • People who saw the final incident firsthand

The honest takeaway

Not every claim should be fought. Some only need an accurate answer. Fighting a claim you cannot support costs time and rarely changes the result. Contesting the ones the facts support protects your account where it counts.
