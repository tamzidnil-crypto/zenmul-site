Retail deductions / Article 5

Which ones to dispute.

Four questions that sort a deduction report into worth it and not worth it.

Disputing every deduction is as costly as disputing none. Valid deductions come back denied, they waste time, and at Target they can use up the limited tries you have on a deduction. The goal is to dispute the invalid ones, with proof, and let the valid ones go.

Question 1: Is it actually wrong?

Some deductions are the result of real supplier errors: a late shipment, an unscannable label, a short carton count. Those are valid. If your own records agree with the retailer, the deduction stands, and the useful step is fixing the cause.

Question 2: Can you prove it?

A dispute without documents rarely succeeds. If the purchase order, invoice, advance ship notice, bill of lading or proof of delivery shows the retailer is wrong, the deduction is worth disputing. If the proof is missing, find it first. The five documents

Question 3: Is the window still open?

A deduction past its dispute window is gone, however wrong it was. Sort by deadline and handle the short window ones, such as compliance chargebacks, first.

Question 4: Is it worth the effort?

A very small deduction with hard to find proof may cost more time than it returns. The same small deduction repeated every month is a different matter. It points to one cause worth fixing, and the total can be large.

Four outcomes

  • Invalid, with proof: dispute it.
  • Partial: dispute only the part that is wrong.
  • Missing proof: find the documents before deciding.
  • Valid: accept it, and fix the cause so it stops.

These questions are our working approach, not a retailer rule. They keep disputes focused on the deductions that can actually come back.
