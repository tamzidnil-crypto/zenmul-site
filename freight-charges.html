01 / Where it goes wrong

Demurrage and detention bills break in a few predictable ways.

Most of these are not obvious from the total. They show up when each line is checked against the actual dates.

  • The invoice is missing information the FMC rule requires, or arrives too late.
  • Free time counted wrong, so billing starts too early.
  • The top tier rate charged on every day instead of the tier that applies.
  • Days the container could not move, because of holds, closed gates or no return appointments, still on the bill.
  • Charges for the same container billed twice.
02 / The rules

For US ocean shipments, the billing rules are on your side.

The Federal Maritime Commission sets what a demurrage or detention invoice must contain and when it must be sent, in 46 CFR Part 541.

  • If an invoice is missing any of the required minimum information, there is no obligation to pay that charge (46 CFR 541.5).
  • If the invoice is issued more than 30 calendar days after the charge was last incurred, you are not required to pay it (46 CFR 541.7).
  • You have at least 30 calendar days from the invoice to request a refund, waiver or reduction (46 CFR 541.8).
  • In April 2026, a federal appeals court upheld the FMC's finding that detention charged while the Port of Savannah was closed for three days was unreasonable (Evergreen v. FMC).

This is a plain summary of the published rules, not legal advice. Read the guide: detention vs demurrage

03 / Lookback audit

Invoices you already paid are not necessarily closed.

In a rule published September 1, 2026, the FMC confirmed that charge complaints have no statute of limitations, as long as the charge was assessed on or after June 16, 2022. The same rule confirms the carrier carries the burden of proving its charge was reasonable.

A lookback audit goes through the demurrage and detention you have already paid since June 16, 2022, and sorts each charge by whether it is worth raising.

You decide what to file. I prepare the file.

  • Your free time terms, from your contract or the carrier's tariff, if you have them.
  • Any emails about terminal closures, holds or missing return appointments, if you have them.

07 / Questions

Common questions

What does this cover?

Demurrage and detention on US ocean shipments, billed by ocean carriers, NVOCCs or marine terminals. Trucker and chassis bills are checked too, against your rate sheet.

I already paid. Is it too late?

For charges assessed on or after June 16, 2022, a charge complaint can still go to the FMC with no filing deadline. Whether a charge is worth raising depends on the amount and the evidence, and that is what the audit tells you.

Will you contact the carrier for me?

No. I prepare the dispute file and you send it. I never negotiate on your behalf and never need your carrier portal login.

What if I do not have my contract terms?

Send what you have. Many checks only need the invoice and the container dates. Anything that cannot be checked without the contract gets marked missing proof, not guessed.

How much does it cost?

The first bill is checked free. After that, it depends on how many invoices there are. You get the cost before any work starts.
