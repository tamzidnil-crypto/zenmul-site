01 / Where it goes wrong

A corrected LTL bill can raise the price weeks after pickup.

Carriers reweigh and reinspect freight at their terminals. When their numbers differ from your bill of lading, a new bill follows. Some of those changes do not hold up.

  • A reweigh with no scale ticket or inspection certificate behind it.
  • A reclass with no inspection record, or measurements that do not match yours.
  • An inspection fee charged when the weight and class did not change.
  • A rebill for extra charges sent long after the original bill.
  • The same PRO number billed twice, or your agreed rate and FAK terms not applied.
02 / The rules

Federal law puts a clock on both sides.

The billing dispute rules for motor carriers are in 49 U.S.C. 13710, and the claim handling rules are in 49 CFR Part 378.

  • A shipper must contest a bill within 180 days of receiving it to keep the right to contest those charges (49 U.S.C. 13710(a)(3)(B)).
  • A carrier must bill extra charges within 180 days of the shipper receiving the original bill to keep the right to collect them (49 U.S.C. 13710(a)(3)(A)).
  • On request, a carrier must give you a copy of the rate, classification and rules your charge is based on (49 U.S.C. 13710(a)(1)).
  • Once you file a written overcharge claim, the carrier must acknowledge it within 30 days and pay, decline or settle it within 60 days, with reasons if it declines (49 CFR 378.7 and 378.8).

This is a plain summary of the published law, not legal advice. Read the guide: the 180 day rule

03 / The class changes

The class rules changed in 2025, and again in 2026.

Since July 19, 2025, a large share of LTL items is classed on a 13 step density scale. More item changes took effect on May 23, 2026, and the next set takes effect on December 12, 2026.

Each shipment is checked against the rules in force on its ship date. Small measuring differences can now move the class.

Some reclasses come from carrier errors. Some come from an out of date class on your own bill of lading. The review tells you which, bill by bill. Read the guide

07 / Questions

Common questions

What does this cover?

Reweigh and reclass charges, inspection fees, late rebills and related billing errors on US LTL shipments billed by motor carriers.

Is every corrected bill worth disputing?

No. Some reweighs and reclasses are correct. Those are marked clearly so you do not spend time on disputes that will be denied.

I booked through a broker. Does this still work?

Yes. The dispute usually goes to the broker first, under your agreement with them. The letter is written for whoever billed you.

Will you contact the carrier for me?

No. I prepare the letters and you send them. I never negotiate on your behalf and never need your carrier portal login.

What if I do not have pickup photos or measurements?

Send what you have. Many checks only need the bill, the bill of lading and the carrier's proof. Anything that cannot be checked is marked missing proof, not guessed.

How much does it cost?

The first bill is checked free. After that, it depends on how many bills there are. You get the cost before any work starts.
