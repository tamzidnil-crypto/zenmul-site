01 / The clocks

Miss the window and a wrong charge becomes a paid charge.

Federal rules give you real rights on freight bills, but each one runs on a deadline. Small importers usually find out after it has passed.

  • Demurrage and detention: the carrier must give you at least 30 days from the invoice to request a refund, waiver or reduction (46 CFR 541.8).
  • Demurrage and detention: an invoice issued more than 30 days after the charge was last incurred does not have to be paid (46 CFR 541.7).
  • LTL freight bills: you must contest a bill within 180 days of receiving it to keep the right to dispute it (49 U.S.C. 13710).
  • Already paid demurrage or detention since June 16, 2022 can still go to the FMC as a charge complaint, with no filing deadline under the September 2026 rule.

Plain summaries of published rules, not legal advice.

09 / Questions

Common questions

My forwarder already checks my bills. Why would I need this?

Your forwarder works for the shipment, and often bills you too. A second check from someone with no stake in the invoice is the point. If the first bill comes back clean, you have lost nothing.

What do I need to send?

The bill itself, plus whatever you have on dates: arrival notice, pickup and empty return dates, your rate sheet or quote. Anything missing gets asked for, not guessed.

Will you contact the carrier for me?

No. I prepare the dispute file and you send it. Nothing goes out in your name unless you send it.

How much does it cost?

The first bill is checked free. After that, it depends on how many bills you have each month. You get the cost before any work starts.

What if there is nothing wrong?

Then I tell you that. A correct charge gets marked correct, so you only dispute what is worth disputing.

Are you a lawyer or a customs broker?

No. I review freight bills and prepare dispute files. I do not handle customs entries, duties or tariff refunds, and I do not give legal advice.
